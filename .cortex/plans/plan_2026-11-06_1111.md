## Design decisions (the "best UX" framing)

The core insight: "Change payment assignment" is the wrong mental model for a top-up. The admin's goal is "get this member to add more money". So the Payment tab becomes the **investment funding** surface:

1. **Header summary strip** (flag-gated): "N active tranches · total principal ₹X · 1 payment under review" — gives immediate context before acting. Falls back to the plain assignment view when the flag is off.
2. **Context-aware action button**: 
   - No assignment → "Assign payment" (unchanged).
   - Has assignment, member has ≥1 ACTIVE tranche, flag ON → **"Add investment (top-up)"**.
   - Has assignment, otherwise → "Change assignment" (unchanged behavior).
3. **Pending-proof guard**: if any investment is `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, the top-up/change action is **disabled** with an inline explanation + a link to the Payment proofs queue ("review the pending proof first"). This is a hard client-side guard because re-assigning mid-review desyncs `expectedAmount` on the review row. (Not just a confirm — disabling prevents the mistake entirely.)
4. **Dialog reframing when in top-up mode**: title "Add investment", description explains the member will be asked to pay this amount on top of their existing ₹Y principal, submitLabel "Assign top-up", confirm text restates it's a *new* tranche, not a change to existing ones. When not in top-up mode, the dialog keeps its current KYC/change wording.

Out of scope (deliberately): editing the shared dialog internals, the KYC approval path, and the mobile app.
